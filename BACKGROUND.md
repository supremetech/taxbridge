# TaxBridge — Business Background

> Updated to reflect Vietnam's legal and regulatory context as of September 2026.

## 1. Project Overview

**TaxBridge** is an AI assistant that helps Vietnamese household businesses record and reconcile daily transactions through natural forms of interaction:

- Entering transactions by text or voice.
- Capturing images of invoices, sales receipts, or expense documents.
- Capturing bank transfer confirmations or importing bank transaction data.
- Using AI to extract information and create draft transactions for user review.
- Matching incoming payments with the corresponding sales orders.
- Detecting unmatched, duplicate, or incomplete transactions.
- Helping users review and “close the day,” thereby creating a traceable revenue ledger.

TaxBridge is more than an accounting data-entry tool. It bridges the gap between how household businesses actually operate—through conversations, messages, images, cash, and bank transfers—and the new requirements for revenue recording, electronic invoicing, and self-declared taxation.

## 2. The 2026 Legal and Regulatory Context

### 2.1. Discontinuation of the Presumptive Tax Method

From January 1, 2026, household businesses and individual business operators are no longer subject to the presumptive tax method. Instead, taxpayers must transition to **self-declaration, self-assessment, and self-payment based on actual revenue**.

This change directly affects how household businesses manage their daily operations. Previously, many businesses paid a relatively fixed assessed amount and had little incentive to record every transaction. From 2026 onward, declared revenue must be reconcilable with sales data, invoices, bank accounts, e-wallets, and information reported by digital platforms.

### 2.2. Tax-Exempt Revenue Threshold

Under the regulations updated in 2026, household businesses and individual business operators with total annual revenue of **no more than VND 1 billion**:

- Are not subject to value-added tax (VAT).
- Are not required to pay personal income tax (PIT) on their business activities.

The initial threshold under Decree No. 68/2026/ND-CP was VND 500 million. Decree No. 141/2026/ND-CP subsequently increased it to VND 1 billion, effective from January 1, 2026. Guidance issued earlier in 2026 using the VND 500 million threshold therefore no longer reflects the final updated rule.

Exemption from VAT and PIT does not eliminate the need for recordkeeping. Businesses must still track revenue to demonstrate eligibility for the exemption and determine when they cross the threshold.

### 2.3. Electronic Invoices

Household businesses and individual business operators with annual revenue **above VND 1 billion** must use either:

- Electronic invoices authenticated by the tax authority; or
- Electronic invoices generated from point-of-sale cash registers connected to the tax authority's data system.

If a business did not exceed the threshold in the preceding year but its cumulative revenue exceeds VND 1 billion during the current year, it must register for electronic invoicing within the statutory period—generally within 30 days from the last day of the tax period in which cumulative revenue exceeded the threshold.

A household business with multiple locations uses a common tax identification number, but each location must be correctly identified on its invoices. Transaction data must therefore record the relevant store, sales channel, and transaction time.

### 2.4. Books, Accounts, and Transaction Data

Household businesses must maintain and retain records appropriate to the scale of their operations. Important practical requirements include:

- Tracking revenue from the sale of goods and provision of services.
- Aggregating revenue from all stores and sales channels, not only cash sales.
- Managing revenue received through bank transfers, e-wallets, e-commerce marketplaces, and digital platforms.
- Reporting the bank accounts and e-wallet identifiers used for business activities as required for tax administration.
- Retaining accounting records for at least the legally prescribed period.
- Separating revenue by business activity when the business conducts activities subject to different tax rates.

Business license tax was discontinued from 2026. However, removing this fee does not reduce the need to control actual revenue, which remains the basis for VAT, PIT, and electronic-invoice obligations.

### 2.5. Transactions Through Digital Platforms

For business conducted through e-commerce marketplaces or digital platforms with payment functionality, the platform may be required to withhold, declare, and pay tax on behalf of the seller in cases prescribed by law. Household businesses must still reconcile platform data against:

- Orders sold.
- Amounts actually paid by customers.
- Platform fees and deductions.
- Net amounts received in the business's account.
- Refunds, cancellations, and adjustments.

This increases the need for a unified transaction data source instead of fragmented management across messages, notebooks, banking applications, and individual sales platforms.

## 3. Problems Faced by Household Businesses

Most small household businesses operate through informal workflows:

1. Receiving orders through face-to-face conversations, phone calls, Zalo, Facebook, or e-commerce platforms.
2. Remembering orders, writing them in notebooks, or leaving them in chat histories.
3. Receiving cash or screenshots of bank transfers.
4. Manually checking at the end of the day which customers have paid and which orders have been completed.
5. Attempting only at month-end to consolidate revenue for tax filing or to send it to an accountant.

This creates several risks:

- Revenue may be omitted or transactions recorded more than once.
- Incoming transfers may not be linked to the correct orders.
- Personal and business funds may be mixed.
- Revenue cannot be reconciled across stores, banks, and sales platforms.
- Evidence may be insufficient to explain declared revenue or determine whether the VND 1 billion threshold has been crossed.
- Data must be entered again into invoicing software or by an accountant.
- Business owners may avoid conventional accounting software because its interface and terminology are too complex.

The market gap is not simply a lack of accounting software. It is the gap between **the moment a transaction actually occurs** and **the moment its data becomes a structured, tax-ready record**.

## 4. Business Needs Addressed by TaxBridge

TaxBridge focuses on the pre-accounting data layer: capturing, normalizing, reconciling, and confirming transactions within the same day.

### 4.1. Easy Capture at the Source

Users do not need to understand journal entries or tax forms. They can speak or type naturally, for example:

> “Sold Ms. Lan two cases of water for VND 480,000. She has already paid by bank transfer.”

AI converts the information into a structured draft containing the customer, goods, amount, payment method, time, and status. The user remains responsible for reviewing and confirming the final data.

### 4.2. Converting Images into Structured Transaction Evidence

TaxBridge can read images of invoices, receipts, or bank transfer confirmations, extract the relevant data, and link the original file to the transaction record. This reduces manual entry while retaining source evidence for later review.

### 4.3. Matching Incoming Payments with Sales Orders

The system proposes matches between incoming payments and orders based on amount, time, transfer description, payer, and customer data. Uncertain cases must be placed in a review queue for user confirmation rather than automatically treated as fully matched.

### 4.4. Day Closing

At the end of each day, TaxBridge presents a short review workflow covering:

- Total orders and expected revenue.
- Cash and bank-transfer revenue.
- Unpaid or partially paid orders.
- Incoming funds not yet matched to an order.
- Potential duplicate transactions.
- Missing documents or low-confidence AI-extracted data.

Once the user resolves exceptions and confirms the records, the business day is closed. Any subsequent amendment should be recorded in a change history to maintain traceability.

### 4.5. Threshold Monitoring and Tax Data Preparation

Using confirmed data, TaxBridge can:

- Track cumulative annual revenue against the VND 1 billion threshold.
- Alert the business when it is approaching or has exceeded the threshold.
- Aggregate revenue by reporting period, location, and business activity.
- Export data to accountants or electronic-invoicing software.
- Help users prepare filing data without replacing the professional judgment of accountants or tax authorities.

## 5. TaxBridge's Business Value

### For Household Business Owners

- Less time spent recording transactions and reconciling accounts at day-end.
- Fewer losses and discrepancies between orders and amounts actually received.
- A clearer view of actual revenue and obligations triggered by crossing the threshold.
- Data ready for accountants, tax authorities, or electronic-invoice providers.
- A familiar conversational interface instead of complex accounting software.

### For Accountants and Tax Service Providers

- Structured data linked to source documents.
- Less time spent asking clients for clarification or re-entering data at month-end.
- Greater focus on exception handling and compliance review instead of transaction collection.

### For the Software Ecosystem

TaxBridge can become an integration layer connecting real-world sales operations with banks, e-wallets, sales platforms, electronic-invoicing software, and accounting systems.

## 6. Product Design Implications

1. **AI creates drafts only:** all AI-extracted or inferred data should carry a pending-confirmation status, a confidence level, and source provenance.
2. **Incoming money must not automatically be treated as revenue:** a transfer may represent a loan, capital contribution, refund, or personal transaction. The user must confirm its nature.
3. **Evidence and history must be retained:** preserve the source image, extracted data, amendments, confirming user, and day-closing time.
4. **Duplicate prevention is essential:** use multiple attributes to identify the same transaction received through text, images, and bank connections.
5. **Multiple locations and channels must be supported:** revenue is aggregated at the household-business level but must remain traceable by store, channel, and business activity.
6. **Policies must not be hard-coded:** revenue thresholds, forms, tax rates, and invoicing rules may change. The system should manage them by version and effective date.
7. **Financial data must be protected:** invoice images, customer information, and bank transactions are sensitive and require access controls, encryption, and clear retention and deletion policies.
8. **Corrections must remain possible:** users need to amend transactions after closing a day, but every change must be captured in an audit log.

## 7. Scope and Compliance Boundaries

TaxBridge should be positioned as a transaction-recording, reconciliation, and data-preparation tool—not, by default, as a tax advisory service or licensed tax agent.

The product should not:

- Conclusively classify incoming money as revenue without sufficient evidence.
- Submit tax returns or issue invoices without appropriate authorization and confirmation.
- Guarantee that system-generated tax estimates always represent the final tax liability.
- Replace review by the taxpayer, accountant, or tax professional in complex cases.

Tax-related content displayed by the system should identify the applicable regulatory version and last-updated date. Before implementing tax filing, tax payment, or official electronic-invoice integrations, the project should undergo a separate review by qualified Vietnamese tax and legal professionals.

## 8. Product Opportunity

The transition from presumptive taxation to actual-revenue-based administration creates a large-scale new need: millions of household businesses must develop structured transaction-recording practices, while most do not want to become professional accounting software users.

TaxBridge's opportunity is to build a **daily financial operations assistant** that begins with the simplest user behaviors—speaking, messaging, and taking photos—and progressively produces data clean enough for reconciliation, invoicing, and tax filing.

Its core competitive advantage lies not only in OCR or chatbot capabilities, but in creating a trustworthy data chain:

> Source document or spoken statement → structured draft → user confirmation → payment-to-order matching → exception handling → day closing → traceable revenue ledger.
